How I help

Three kinds of help, often in the same person at the same time

Job search

Get seen by the people who hire

Most applications vanish because they look like the other 900. I show you how recruiters actually screen, then build a search that reaches hiring managers directly.

  • Target list of employers worth your time
  • Outreach messages that get replies
  • Resume and LinkedIn written for a recruiter's 30-second scan
  • Interview prep and salary negotiation
Direction in the AI era

Figure out what's next, and what lasts

AI is changing which jobs exist and what they ask of people. We work out what you want, which of your skills carry over, and where they'll still be valuable in five years.

  • Strengths and interests, mapped to real roles
  • Transferable-skills map for your pivot
  • Short experiments to test a direction before committing
  • Using AI tools well, and knowing where they mislead
Calm and joy

Stay steady through the search

A long search wears people down: the silence, the rejections, the money worry. We build a rhythm you can sustain, and look for the parts of work that actually make you happy.

  • A weekly routine with real stopping points
  • Handling rejection and long silences
  • Networking that works for introverts
  • Finding meaning in the job you take next

The recruiter's view

Signal, not noise

From the hiring side, most job searches are noise: hundreds of identical applications nobody reads. A good search sends fewer, clearer signals to the right people. That idea runs through everything I teach.

About

Hi, I'm Tom

For six years I was a senior recruiter and recruitment manager, screening candidates and watching which ones got hired, and why. Before that I was a corporate trainer and coach; after it, a program manager at companies including Uber and Cisco. My master's is in counseling psychology.

Since 2021 I've coached people through job searches and career changes, and helped design the coaching approach behind an AI job-search platform. I wrote Zen and the Art of Job Hunting because most job-search advice ignores how stressful the process is. I think you search better when you're steadier.
